# Vogue Design School

## 1. Philosophy
Avant-garde elegance, visual supremacy, and high-fashion editorial. The Vogue design system prioritizes striking photography and expressive typography over rigid usability. It is meant to be browsed, admired, and felt. White space is used not just for separation, but as a framing device for art.

## 2. Exact CSS Design Tokens
**Fonts:**
- Display/Headlines: `Didot, Bodoni MT, serif` (High contrast serifs)
- Body Text: `Helvetica Neue, sans-serif` or elegant serifs.

**Hex Colors:**
- Background: `#FFFFFF` (Stark White) or `#FDFDFD`
- Text: `#000000` (Pure Black for maximum contrast)
- Accents: `#E50000` (Vogue Red) or subtle neutrals (`#F4F4F4`).

## 3. Spatial Reasoning & Grid
- **Grid System:** Broken, asymmetrical grids. Overlapping elements. Elements break out of containers to create visual tension.
- **Spacing:** Massive margins. "Airy" layouts where negative space equals luxury.
- **Layout:** Art-directed pages, distinct from standard web templates.

## 4. Component Architecture & Physics
- **Components:** Full-bleed imagery, thin elegant lines, oversized drop caps.
- **Physics:** Smooth, cinematic scrolling. Parallax effects and slow fades that mimic the turning of a glossy magazine page.

## 5. Anti-Slop Validation Checklist
- [ ] Is the primary display typeface a high-contrast serif (like Didot)?
- [ ] Is photography given absolute priority over UI elements?
- [ ] Is there an abundance of negative space conveying luxury?
- [ ] Are grid alignments intentionally broken for artistic effect?
- [ ] Does it feel more like a glossy magazine than a software tool?
